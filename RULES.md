# Major Revisions: rules

A deduction card game about peer review. One player is the **Author**, who secretly plants one or two flaws in a
five-card manuscript. The others are **Reviewers**, who spend a limited number of review requests to find the flawed
card and give the verdict an editor would. 3–6 players, 20–30 minutes a manuscript. A solo version is in the app.

## Components

| Deck | Cards | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Study | 8 | A fictional study: field, design, the authors' claim and the reporting guideline that applies (CONSORT 2025, STROBE, PRISMA 2020, COREQ, STARD 2015 or ARRIVE 2.0). |
| Manuscript | 78 | Each study's five parts (Question, Method, Sample, Measurement, Analysis), each in a **sound** version and, for most parts, a **flawed** version. |
| Request | 24 | What a reviewer can ask for: flow diagram, registration, blinding, missing data, coding tree… Each names the card it is asked of and the guideline items that require it. |
| Verdict | 4 | Accept, Minor revisions, Major revisions, Reject. Each player needs one set (print one per player). |
| Reference | 3 | The five cards, a turn summary, and scoring. |

The **flaw key** (`print/answer-key.html`) lists every planted flaw, its verdict, the request that catches it and why
it matters. The Author may keep it at hand.

### Reading the cards

- **Manuscript card.** Top: the part of the study and the study's title, then **Sound** or **Flaw · minor / major /
  reject**. Under *As submitted* is what the paper says about this part; everyone hears it. Below are one or two
  answers, each labelled with the request it answers. On a flawed card, the flawed answer is marked ⚠ and the
  planted flaw is named. Only the Author ever sees the front during play.
- **Request card.** The card it is asked of (its colour and badge), what it asks, the flaws it can catch, and the
  checklist items that require it, e.g. *CONSORT 22a–b · STROBE 13a–c · PRISMA 16a*.
- **Verdict card.** What the verdict means and when to give it.

### The verdict rule

A manuscript's verdict is that of its **worst** flaw: Reject beats Major revisions beats Minor revisions. A
manuscript with no flaw (expert variant only) is accepted.

- **Minor revisions:** a gap in reporting or wording; the conclusion stands once it is fixed.
- **Major revisions:** a real problem that the authors can still fix, with new analyses, data they already hold, or a
  redone step (for example re-measuring stored brain slices blind).
- **Reject:** the data themselves are compromised (predictable allocation, a self-selected sample, interviews by the
  participants' manager); only a new study could answer the question.

## Setting up

1. Choose an Author. Everyone else is a Reviewer. Give each Reviewer a set of four Verdict cards.
2. The Author picks a Study card, places it face up, and takes that study's ten or so Manuscript cards.
3. For each of the five parts, the Author secretly chooses the sound or the flawed version, so that **one or two**
   are flawed, and lays the five cards face down in order: Question, Method, Sample, Measurement, Analysis.
4. The Author reads the claim on the Study card and, card by card, each *As submitted* line aloud.
5. Shuffle the Request cards and lay **8** face up in a row: the request menu. (With the 24 Requests, some will not
   apply to this study; spotting which do is part of the game.)

## Playing

The Reviewers share **6 review rounds** (keep count with a coin on the Study card). Starting left of the Author,
each Reviewer in turn does **one** of these:

- **Ask.** Take a Request from the menu and read it out. The Author looks for the face-down card that answers it
  (the card named on the Request) and reads aloud **only that answer**, truthfully and word for word. If no card
  answers it, the Author says "Not applicable". Either way the round is spent and the Request is discarded; refill
  the menu from the deck.
- **Report.** Point at one face-down card and say "flawed". The Author answers only "yes" or "no". On a yes, the
  Reviewer scores and the Author turns that card face up. On a no, the Reviewer loses a point. A report does not
  spend a round.

The review ends when all flaws are found or the rounds run out. Then every Reviewer secretly chooses a Verdict card
and all reveal together. The Author turns every card face up and reads the flaw key's *why* for each flaw.

## Scoring

| Who | For | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Reviewer | Reporting a flawed card | +3, plus 1 for each review round still left |
| Reviewer | Reporting a sound card | −1 |
| Reviewer | The right verdict | +2 |
| Author | Each flaw no one reported | +3 |

Pass the Author role to the left. Play until everyone has been Author once; highest total wins.

## Variants

- **Expert.** The Author may plant **no** flaw. Reviewers who accept a sound manuscript score +2 as usual, and
  every report on it scores −1.
- **Classroom.** One Author (the teacher), the class in teams of Reviewers, one shared request menu. Afterwards, look
  up the checklist item of each request that caught a flaw.
- **Solo (the app).** The computer deals a manuscript from a number (the same number always deals the same
  manuscript, so a group can review one together). You get 12 Requests, two of which don't apply to the study, and
  five rounds. Then mark the flawed card or cards and give a verdict. Scoring: +3 per flaw found, −1 per sound card
  marked, +2 for the right verdict, and +1 per unused request if your whole report is right.
